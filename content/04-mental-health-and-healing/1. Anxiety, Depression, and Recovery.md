Mental health and addiction are often connected. Some people begin using substances to escape anxiety, sadness, painful thoughts, or emotional exhaustion. Substance use may provide temporary relief, but it can also make those problems worse over time.

Recovery may also bring emotions back to the surface. When a person is no longer using a substance to avoid or numb difficult feelings, anxiety and depression may become more noticeable.

> Mental health struggles do not mean recovery is failing. They may be signs that another part of healing needs attention.

## Understanding Anxiety

Feeling nervous or worried sometimes is a normal part of life. Anxiety becomes more concerning when the worry does not go away, becomes difficult to control, or interferes with work, relationships, sleep, and normal responsibilities.

Possible signs of anxiety include:

- Constant or excessive worry
    
- Feeling restless or unable to relax
    
- Irritability
    
- Difficulty concentrating
    
- Trouble sleeping
    
- Muscle tension
    
- Racing thoughts
    
- Feeling that something bad is about to happen
    
- Avoiding situations because of fear
    

The National Institute of Mental Health explains that anxiety disorders involve more than occasional worry and may become worse over time without proper support (National Institute of Mental Health [NIMH], 2024a).

Anxiety can also become one of the internal triggers discussed in [[1. Identifying Personal Triggers|Identifying Personal Triggers]].

## Understanding Depression

Depression is more than having a bad day or temporarily feeling sad. It may affect how a person thinks, feels, sleeps, eats, works, and completes everyday activities.

Possible signs of depression include:

- Ongoing sadness or emptiness
    
- Hopelessness
    
- Irritability
    
- Loss of interest in normal activities
    
- Low energy
    
- Difficulty concentrating
    
- Changes in sleep or appetite
    
- Feelings of guilt or worthlessness
    
- Withdrawing from other people
    
- Thoughts about death or suicide
    

Depression can affect anyone and may range from mild to severe (NIMH, 2024b).

## The Connection to Recovery

Anxiety and depression can make recovery more difficult when they are ignored. A person may begin thinking that returning to substance use is the only way to feel calm, sleep, or escape emotional pain.

However, using substances to manage mental health symptoms usually provides only temporary relief. It may create additional problems involving health, relationships, responsibilities, and shame.

Addressing both mental health and substance use can help a person better understand the full situation instead of focusing on only one part of it.

This is also connected to [[3. Trauma and Addiction - Understanding the Connection|Trauma and Addiction: Understanding the Connection]] and [[2. PTSD and Trauma Recovery|PTSD and Trauma Recovery]].

## Helpful Forms of Support

Different people may benefit from different approaches, including:

- Counseling or psychotherapy
    
- Support groups
    
- Medical care
    
- Medication prescribed by a qualified provider
    
- Regular sleep and healthy routines
    
- Exercise
    
- Honest conversations with trusted people
    
- Reducing isolation
    
- Prayer or spiritual support
    
- Developing healthier ways to handle stress
    

The daily habits discussed in [[2. Healthy Habits That Support Recovery|Healthy Habits That Support Recovery]] can support mental health, but they are not always a replacement for professional care.

A person should not stop or change prescribed mental health medication without speaking with the provider who prescribed it.

## Pay Attention to Warning Signs

It may be time to seek professional help when anxiety or depression:

- Continues for several weeks
    
- Interferes with work or school
    
- Damages relationships
    
- Makes normal responsibilities difficult
    
- Leads to increased substance use
    
- Causes severe isolation
    
- Creates thoughts of self-harm or suicide
    

More information about recognizing these warning signs can be found in [[3. Managing Stress and Knowing When to Seek Help|Managing Stress and Knowing When to Seek Help]].

## Reflection

I believe mental health has to be taken seriously during recovery. Removing a substance does not automatically remove the anxiety, depression, trauma, or stress that may have been underneath it. Asking for help with those problems is not weakness. In many cases, it is part of building a stronger and more lasting recovery.
