Spiritual growth can become an important part of recovery. For Christians, recovery is not only about controlling behavior. It may also involve rebuilding trust in God, receiving forgiveness, developing humility, and learning to live according to faith.

This growth does not usually happen through one emotional moment. Like the habits discussed in [[2. Healthy Habits That Support Recovery|Healthy Habits That Support Recovery]], spiritual growth is often supported by small practices repeated over time.

> Faith does not mean pretending to be strong. It can begin with honestly admitting the need for help.

## Prayer

Prayer allows a person to speak honestly with God. It does not need to sound impressive or follow a perfect format.

Prayer during recovery may include:

- Asking for strength during cravings
    
- Admitting fear, anger, or confusion
    
- Asking for forgiveness
    
- Thanking God for progress
    
- Praying for damaged relationships
    
- Asking for wisdom before a decision
    
- Praying for someone else who is struggling
    
- Sitting quietly when words are difficult
    

Prayer can also create a pause between a trigger and a response. However, prayer should not be treated as an excuse to ignore medical care, counseling, or other support when those are needed.

## Reading and Applying Scripture

Scripture can provide encouragement, correction, and direction. Reading a small passage consistently may be more useful than attempting an unrealistic plan that is quickly abandoned.

Helpful themes to study may include:

- Hope
    
- Forgiveness
    
- Self-control
    
- Endurance
    
- Wisdom
    
- Anger
    
- Anxiety
    
- Temptation
    
- Community
    
- Service
    
- Identity in Christ
    

James 1 discusses perseverance during trials. First Corinthians 10:13 addresses temptation and the possibility of a way through it. Romans 12:2 focuses on renewal of the mind.

The goal is not only to read Scripture but to ask how it should affect choices, relationships, and behavior.

## Stay Connected to Other Believers

Isolation can harm both recovery and spiritual health. A church community may provide friendship, prayer, teaching, accountability, and opportunities to serve.

Healthy spiritual support might come from:

- A pastor
    
- A small group
    
- A mature Christian mentor
    
- A recovery ministry
    
- A church counselor
    
- Trusted friends
    
- A men’s or women’s group
    
- A Bible study
    

The relationships discussed in [[3. Finding Community in Recovery|Finding Community in Recovery]] can help prevent faith from becoming something practiced entirely alone.

Church communities are made of imperfect people, so wisdom and boundaries are still necessary. A person should look for support that encourages honesty, responsibility, compassion, and appropriate professional care.

## Practice Confession and Accountability

Spiritual growth requires honesty. Hiding struggles may allow shame and temptation to become stronger.

James 5:16 connects confession and prayer with healing. This does not mean every private detail should be shared publicly. It means having trustworthy people who can provide prayer, truth, and accountability.

The principles in [[3. Accountability and Goal Setting in Recovery|Accountability and Goal Setting in Recovery]] also apply here:

- Speak honestly before a crisis
    
- Admit when a boundary has been crossed
    
- Ask for prayer
    
- Accept correction
    
- Follow through with practical changes
    
- Seek additional help when needed
    

## Serve Others Carefully

Serving others can provide purpose and remind a person that recovery has value beyond personal comfort.

Service might include:

- Helping at church
    
- Supporting a recovery meeting
    
- Volunteering in the community
    
- Encouraging someone who is struggling
    
- Helping family members
    
- Sharing a personal story responsibly
    
- Mentoring others after developing stable recovery
    

However, a person should not feel pressured to become everyone’s counselor. Early recovery may require focusing on personal stability before taking responsibility for someone else’s recovery.

## Faith and Professional Treatment Can Work Together

Spiritual support and professional treatment do not have to compete with each other. Recovery can involve faith, counseling, medical treatment, medication, peer support, and family support.

SAMHSA identifies faith-based approaches as one possible part of an individualized recovery process (Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration [SAMHSA], 2024). Research has also found that spirituality can play a helpful role in recovery for many people, although its role differs from person to person (Kelly & Eddie, 2020).

A person can pray while also attending therapy. They can trust God while also following medical advice. Seeking help does not necessarily show a lack of faith.

## A Simple Spiritual Routine

A manageable routine might include:

-  Begin the day with a short prayer.
    
-  Read a small section of Scripture.
    
-  Write down one thought or lesson.
    
-  Contact a trusted believer during difficult moments.
    
-  Attend church or a small group regularly.
    
-  Practice gratitude.
    
-  Serve someone in a healthy and realistic way.
    
-  Be honest about struggles rather than hiding them.
    

## Reflection

Faith has become an important part of how I understand recovery. I do not believe that spiritual growth means never struggling or always having the right words. For me, it means continuing to turn toward God, accepting help, being honest, and allowing my beliefs to affect the way I treat my family, handle responsibilities, and respond to difficult situations.
