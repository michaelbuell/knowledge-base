Books, apps, and podcasts can provide education and encouragement between counseling appointments, meetings, or conversations with trusted people.

These tools should not be expected to replace medical care, treatment, or human support. They are most useful when they support the plan created in [[1. Building A Personal Recovery Plan|Building a Personal Recovery Plan]].

> A resource is helpful when it leads toward healthy action, not when collecting information becomes a replacement for taking action.

## Books to Consider

### _Alcoholics Anonymous_

Often called the “Big Book,” this is the main text used in Alcoholics Anonymous. It contains an explanation of the AA program along with personal recovery stories.

This book may be most useful for someone interested in AA or the Twelve Step approach.

### _Narcotics Anonymous_

Often called the “Basic Text,” this book explains the Narcotics Anonymous program and includes experiences from people recovering from drug addiction.

It may be helpful for someone considering NA meetings or wanting to understand the organization’s approach.

### _Atomic Habits_ by James Clear

This is not specifically a recovery book, but it explains how repeated small actions, routines, and environmental changes can contribute to larger personal change.

Its ideas connect closely with [[2. Healthy Habits That Support Recovery|Healthy Habits That Support Recovery]] and [[3. Accountability and Goal Setting in Recovery|Accountability and Goal Setting in Recovery]].

### _The Body Keeps the Score_ by Bessel van der Kolk

This book examines the effects of trauma on the brain and body. It may help readers better understand the connection discussed in [[3. Trauma and Addiction - Understanding the Connection|Trauma and Addiction: Understanding the Connection]] and [[2. PTSD and Trauma Recovery|PTSD and Trauma Recovery]].

Some of the material discusses traumatic experiences and may be emotionally difficult. Readers should take breaks or discuss the content with a counselor if necessary.

## Helpful Apps

### Meeting Guide

Meeting Guide is the official free AA meeting-finder app. It uses meeting information provided by local AA service organizations and helps users locate nearby meetings and resources (Alcoholics Anonymous World Services, Inc., n.d.).

### SMART Recovery App

The SMART Recovery app includes a meeting finder and tools related to motivation, urges, thoughts, and balanced living. It can also connect users to online meetings (SMART Recovery, n.d.).

### A Basic Calendar or Notes App

A specialized recovery app is not always necessary. A normal calendar, reminder, or notes app can be used to:

- Track counseling and meeting dates
    
- Save emergency contacts
    
- Record triggers
    
- Write daily goals
    
- Track sobriety milestones
    
- Save reasons for recovery
    
- Set medication or routine reminders
    

Private information should be protected with appropriate phone security.

## Podcasts

### SMART Recovery Podcasts

SMART Recovery publishes conversations about addiction, motivation, self-management, and recovery. These episodes may be useful for people interested in the organization’s practical approach.

### Celebrate Recovery Official Podcast

The Celebrate Recovery Official Podcast includes recovery stories, encouragement, and teaching centered on Celebrate Recovery’s Christian principles (Celebrate Recovery, n.d.).

### Recovery Stories Have Power

Faces & Voices of Recovery produces the _Recovery Stories Have Power_ podcast. It discusses different paths to recovery, recovery leadership, and efforts to reduce stigma (Faces & Voices of Recovery, n.d.).

## Choosing Media Carefully

Not every recovery resource found online is accurate or healthy. Before trusting a book, app, video, or podcast, consider:

- Who created it?
    
- Does it make unrealistic promises?
    
- Is it trying to sell an expensive cure?
    
- Does it discourage medical or mental health treatment?
    
- Does it encourage responsibility and safety?
    
- Is the information based on evidence, lived experience, faith, or personal opinion?
    
- Is that distinction made clear?
    

Personal stories can provide hope, but one person’s experience should not automatically be treated as medical advice.

## Reflection

Books and podcasts have helped me understand ideas in a way that sometimes feels more personal than a short article. At the same time, reading about change is easier than practicing it. I believe these resources are most valuable when they encourage someone to make a plan, contact another person, attend a meeting, or take another healthy step.
