A crisis can make it difficult to think clearly or search for the right information. Important contacts should be saved in a phone and included in [[1. Building A Personal Recovery Plan|Building a Personal Recovery Plan]] before they are needed.

> [!danger] Immediate Danger  
> Call **911** when someone is in immediate danger, cannot be awakened, is having trouble breathing, has a seizure, has a serious injury, or may be experiencing an overdose.

This page lists resources for the United States. Contact information should be reviewed occasionally because services may change.

## 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Call or text:

# **988**

The 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline provides free, confidential support for people experiencing suicidal thoughts, emotional distress, mental health concerns, or substance-use crises. Phone, text, and online chat support are available throughout the United States and its territories (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, n.d.).

A person does not have to wait until they are certain they may attempt suicide before contacting 988. It is appropriate to reach out during serious emotional distress or when concerned about someone else.

## Veterans Crisis Line

Veterans, service members, National Guard and Reserve members, and their loved ones can contact the Veterans Crisis Line.

- **Call 988 and press 1**
    
- **Text 838255**
    
- Use the confidential online chat
    

VA enrollment or VA healthcare eligibility is not required. The service is free, confidential, and available at all times (U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, n.d.).

## SAMHSA National Helpline

Call:

# **1-800-662-HELP (4357)**

SAMHSA’s National Helpline provides free, confidential treatment referrals and information about mental health and substance-use services. It is available in English and Spanish (Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration [SAMHSA], 2023).

This is primarily an information and referral line. Call 911 during an immediate medical emergency or 988 during a mental health or suicide crisis.

## FindTreatment.gov

**FindTreatment.gov** allows users to search for mental health and substance-use treatment providers by location.

It may be used to search for:

- Outpatient treatment
    
- Residential treatment
    
- Mental health services
    
- Substance-use services
    
- Providers offering medications for certain substance use disorders
    

The search tool is confidential and anonymous (SAMHSA, n.d.).

## Poison Control

Call:

# **1-800-222-1222**

Poison Control provides free and confidential expert guidance for possible poisonings, medication errors, or unexpected exposure to a potentially harmful substance.

An online tool is also available through Poison Control. Call 911 immediately if the person collapses, has a seizure, cannot breathe normally, or cannot be awakened (National Capital Poison Center, n.d.).

## Suspected Opioid Overdose

Naloxone is a medication that can reverse an opioid overdose when it is given in time. It may be available as a nasal spray or injection.

During a suspected opioid overdose:

1. Call 911.
    
2. Give naloxone if it is available.
    
3. Follow the dispatcher’s instructions.
    
4. Give another dose if directed or if the person does not respond.
    
5. Stay with the person until emergency help arrives.
    

Naloxone will not harm someone who is overdosing on a substance other than an opioid, so the CDC recommends using it when an opioid overdose is suspected (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC], 2025).

## Reflection

During a crisis, pride, fear, or shame can make someone hesitate to ask for help. I believe it is better to make the call and discover that the situation can be managed than to wait until it becomes too late. Keeping these contacts available is a simple step that could protect a life.
